## summary
on 11 july 2007, at roughly 3:10 to 3:30 pm, nine members of one household walked onto the railway line at kashor, mymensingh, and were struck by a mail train. seven died on impact. two children died on the way to hospital. the youngest was eight.

the household called itself the adam family and followed a belief system invented by its late patriarch, anwar hossain (known locally as anwar darbesh and in the family's writings as "abdul adam"), who claimed to be the original adam. inside the house, police recovered five diaries, long handwritten notes in bengali and english, and a written declaration of the "adam religion."

the standard label is "mass suicide," and the physical and documentary evidence for a planned group act is strong. yet that single phrase hides deep disagreements and major gaps in the record:

- anthropologist and physician nasima selim, who analysed the original notes, argued that the family combined a *be-shara* (anti-orthodox) protest theology with shared psychotic delusion.
- the family's own writing blames six years of violent persecution by neighbours and relatives, naming specific villagers.
- the sole surviving family member, who was in brahmanbaria that day, maintains it was a pre-planned murder rather than suicide.
- two or three of the dead were children aged eight to ten whose near-identical english notes appear prompted by adults, and who could not have meaningfully chosen this.
- checking the primary 2007 and 2019 press reports against selim's 2010 study resolves several mysteries in secondary summaries, including who the live-in disciple "leena" really was, while exposing direct contradictions over who actually died.

this note lays out what is documented in the contemporary reporting and the notes themselves, what is only claimed, and what remains unresolved.

## background
**the patriarch.** anwar hossain, known locally as anwar darbesh ("saint"), was a non-commissioned officer in the pakistan army who retired before bangladesh's independence in 1971. fluent in arabic, urdu, and persian, he was known locally for strict islamic observance until the mid-1990s and held a reputation for clairvoyance and spiritual healing, drawing visitors from outside his district for blessings and remedies.

![house of anwar darbesh](/images/2007/house-of-anwar-fakir.jpg "associated with anwar darbesh | left")

**the money.** after leaving the army, he produced an herbal hair oil, *heem kushum*, advertising it in local cinema halls until it became the family's sole income. in 1985, authorities banned its sale because he had no patent. the family believed jealous neighbours and relatives had instigated the ban. supporting a wife, five daughters, and two sons, he fell into severe poverty, writing later in a diary that they had starved and that he could not even buy clothes for his children.

**the study and the followers.** darbesh turned to comparative religion, reading across islam, christianity, and other faiths. a small circle of followers visited and occasionally lived in the house, offering small donations that helped the household survive.

**the turn.** around 1995 (following a public argument over religion at a local tea stall that ended in him being beaten, according to *barta24*), darbesh began publicly questioning the divine status of the qur'an and the legitimacy of the prophet muhammad. he stopped praying, declared religious rituals useless, and withdrew from village life. his entire household followed him, identifying from that year with what he called the adam religion.

**the doctrine.** as recorded in darbesh's declaration and the family's diaries:

- darbesh ("abdul adam") was the original adam and creator of the universe.
- all organised religions (islam, christianity, hinduism, buddhism) and all mosques, madrasas, temples, and pagodas were illusions.
- muhammad was portrayed as the king of jinns or satan himself ("hinath mokrom ajajil saitan"), and neighbours, relatives, and religious authorities were his "agents."
- every human body was itself divine ("body of allah"), and each family member saw themselves as another body or incarnation of abdul adam.
- darbesh wrote that he was the reincarnation of aynal haq (mansur al-hallaj) of multan, a sufi executed for claiming divinity, and that his soul had passed into his young grandson, moula ("montu").

**the elder son.** in 1993, darbesh's elder son golam mohiuddin abdul kadir (called maehi in english reporting, and identified as arif adam or anwar mahin in *barta24*) moved to dhaka to study and support the household. he abandoned islamic prayer, adopted the alias galen m. sany, and eventually worked as director of admissions at east west university in dhaka, keeping the family afloat financially.

## method
this is a desk study built on direct review of primary-level reporting and academic analysis rather than encyclopedia summaries alone:

1. **nasima selim (2010)**, *"an extraordinary truth? the ādam 'suicide' notes from bangladesh,"* published in *mental health, religion & culture* 13(3), pp. 223–244. selim examined the surviving notebooks, diaries, and wills directly, interviewed neighbours and the surviving daughter, and used pseudonyms for the family members in her paper.
2. **kamran reza chowdhury (2 august 2007)**, *"driven to their deaths,"* a special report in *star weekend magazine* (*the daily star*), which quotes directly from the five diaries seized by police, alongside *the daily star*'s initial 13 july 2007 police report.
3. **jahangir kabir jewel (12 july 2007)**, *"mymensingh joint suicide defies common sense,"* published by *bdnews24.com* the day after the deaths, capturing immediate local, police, and relative reactions (including darbesh's brother, abdul hannan).
4. **osman jafar (12 december 2019)**, *"আদম ধর্ম ও এক পরিবারের ৯ সদস্যের ট্রেনে কাটা পড়ে আত্মহত্যার কাহিনী,"* published by *barta24*, preserving the bangla press roster, survivor meherunnesa mobi's brahmanbaria statement, and eyewitness photographs taken inside the house on 11 july 2007 by local resident abu bakar siddiqui.

limits :

- selim anonymised the family members in her 2010 academic paper (using names like "hazera," "maruf," "adil," "afsari," "baby," "monsura," "razia," "sharmin," "montu," "munni," and "reena"), whereas the press reports used their real names. cross-referencing them resolves crucial facts while exposing direct contradictions over who the ninth victim was.
- where i interpret rather than report, i say so

## results
### the death of the patriarch (2000)
anwar darbesh died of cardiac arrest at birdem hospital in dhaka in july 2000 (recorded as 10 july in english reports and 11 july in *barta24*) following a prolonged illness. he left behind strict, written instructions governing his own burial:

> "after my death, do not perform the janazah. do not give me a bath and do not cover me with a kafan. dig up the soil inside the house and bury me under. bury me in the same clothes i shall be wearing. when you lay me down in the grave, my head should be turned towards the east, my feet to the west, and my face towards south. if there is any violation to this, i will take severe revenge."

orienting a body with the feet pointed west toward makkah directly contravenes islamic funerary practice. when his eldest son (identified as maehi in english reporting and as arif adam in *barta24*) returned the body to mymensingh alongside an associate named tuhin and attempted to bury him inside the family compound, neighbours and relatives intervened, condemning the act as heresy. to justify the non-islamic burial posture, the family claimed they had converted to christianity, a claim local church officials promptly refuted. police and a magistrate stepped in, and darbesh was given an islamic funeral bath, janazah, and burial at char kalibari graveyard in mymensingh. none of his immediate family attended.

this public confrontation marked a decisive rupture. it permanently alienated the household from the surrounding community, and the anniversary of his death became "black day," the exact date around which the family scheduled its own end seven years later.

<br>

### the murder of the eldest son (2004 to 2005)
four years later, the family suffered a second, destabilising blow when its eldest son and primary breadwinner was killed in dhaka. contemporary english accounts state that maehi, living under the alias galen m. sany and serving as director of admissions at east west university, was murdered in september 2004 in a busy commercial district, with police subsequently recovering forged certificates from the university of washington and the university of leicester among his effects. *barta24* and his surviving sister meherunnesa mobi place the killing in 2005 (mobi giving the date 10 september 2005 and referring to him as anwar mahin).

whichever record holds, the consequence was immediate. the household lost its sole financial anchor and its only functional link to the outside world. local resistance resurfaced when his body was brought back to char kalibari graveyard, relenting only after his younger brother apologised to the community and reaffirmed the family's muslim identity.

by the mid-2000s, the household had:
- no commercial livelihood, following the 1985 hair-oil ban
- no patriarch, since july 2000
- no established earner in dhaka, leaving the surviving younger son (recorded as arif, 22, in english accounts and rahat, 22, in *barta24*) to support the household through private tuition or local work
- an unresolved, public history of religious conflict with neighbours
- an increasingly closed theology teaching that outsiders were agents of evil and that physical death meant reunion with "abdul adam"

<br>

### the day (11 july 2007)
the family's home in the itkhola area of kashor stood roughly thirty feet from the railway line near the outer signal of mymensingh station. at roughly 3:10 to 3:30 pm on wednesday, 11 july 2007, the 254 down g-m mail express (running from bahadurabad ghat and jamalpur toward mymensingh under loco masters abdul matin and shah muhammad enayet hossain khan) approached the stretch.

![the family home in kashor](/images/2007/their-home.jpg "the family home near the railway line | left")

![the railway line at kashor](/images/2007/the-railway-line.jpg "the railway corridor near kashor, mymensingh | right")

according to driver testimonies recorded by police and the press, the crew spotted a group emerging from the left side of the embankment and walking along the line, holding onto one another. the drivers sounded the whistle, assuming the group was crossing the track. when they remained motionless on the rails, the crew applied the emergency brakes, too late to halt the train. after impact, the train stopped briefly before the assistant driver moved it forward out of fear of backlash from the rapidly gathering crowd. one of the drivers reportedly fainted at the scene.

seven people died on impact, with remains scattered over nearly three kilometres toward the sankipara level crossing. two children, a young boy and a girl crying out for water, were pulled alive from the tracks by bystanders but died on the way to mymensingh medical college hospital. the severity of the scene triggered widespread panic among hundreds of onlookers, and when no relatives came forward following the 12 july post-mortems, police handed the remains over to the charity anjuman mufidul islam for unclaimed islamic burial, the exact rite anwar darbesh had sought to forbid seven years earlier.

**4. why the lists collide**
- **the ninth body (mobi vs. leena vs. rahat):** once mobi proved she was alive in brahmanbaria, one female body on the initial police list had to be accounted for. *the daily star* (august 2007) and *the financial express* concluded the ninth victim was **leena**, whose diary stated *"they are taking me with them, this is my greatest achievement."* later wire summaries (including *bdnews24* and wikipedia) kept all nine victims as blood relatives, counting both sons arif and rahat, and omitted leena despite quoting her diary.
- **nasima selim's 2010 paper:** analysing the recovered notebooks, selim likewise counts **eight family members plus the 18-year-old live-in disciple or housemaid ("reena" / leena)** and only **two children** (aged 10 and 9), noting that one adult son was not among the dead.
- **which brother died before 2007:** english reports state elder son *maehi* (galen m. sany) was murdered in dhaka in 2004 while *arif* (22) survived until 2007; *barta24* instead identifies *arif adam* (called *anwar mahin* by mobi) as the elder son who died in dhaka in 2005, leaving *rahat* (22) as the sole surviving adult male in the house.
- **shifting ages and parentages:** across the reports, ages jump by up to eight years (shabnam appears as 10, 17, and 18), rahat is recorded both as a son (18, 20, or 22) and a daughter (22), and moula and moon/mariam are attributed either to mobi or to her sisters.

### what the notes actually say
unlike most summaries, selim's paper and the *daily star* extracts preserve the exact voice of the texts.

![warning sign and holding plate](/images/2007/holding-plate.jpg "warning notice and holding plate at the house | left")

![adam cult stone](/images/2007/adam-stone.jpg "stone associated with the adam declaration | right")

**1. the post-2000 spirit contact writings.** in one diary quoted by *the daily star*, darbesh himself had written before his death:

> "i am aadom now living a humble life in mymensingh. we have led a very miserable life and even starved. i could not manage to buy clothes for my children when they needed them. i have taken my son maehi and will take other members to me."

**note**: this line appears in a diary attributed to darbesh after maehi's 2004 death, meaning either a family member was channeling darbesh's voice in first person after 2004, or the press misattributed a spirit possession entry to darbesh himself.

selim confirms that the family held evening sessions to invoke the spirits of darbesh and maehi, slept during the day and stayed awake at night, and treated each living member as an incarnation ("another body") of abdul adam.

![rope setup inside the house](/images/2007/rope-attempt.jpg "ropes hanging inside a room prior to the track deaths | left")

![objects inside the room](/images/2007/attempt-stuffs.jpg "items photographed inside the house | right")

**2. the persecution claims.** entries written after 2000 describe sustained physical terror. one daughter wrote:

> "people used to throw bombs at us at day and night and it rocked our house. we were suffering from mental and physical trauma since my father left. but we got peace and new lease of life when he came to our family through my body. nobody knew my father. they called him ghost (jeen) and heretic."

another entry, attributed to hena, names specific locals:

> "zakir, totun, zia, tuhin, anis, dulal, firoz, feku and other villagers beat up my children and tried to burn my house as my children attempted to bury me [darbesh] as per my instruction. zakir and his associates threatened us to kick out and occupy our house... the villagers attacked my children with sticks, knives, cleavers and other weapons. they threw stones like rain and made holes in the tin shed of the house. rainwater flooded our house... we will soon take revenge."

**3. hostility toward the surviving sister.** selim uncovered a striking detail missing from newspaper summaries: the family's diaries expressed intense anger toward the one surviving sister, meherunnesa mobi (called "baby" in selim's study), referring to her in their theology as *"baby muhammad shaitan"* who had spat on them, insulted their father, and "destroyed our peace of mind." this explains why she was estranged and absent in brahmanbaria on 11 july.

**4. the disciple leena's note.** leena ("reena"), who came to live with the family with her mother and sister in 2000 and served as the household's main link to the local bazaar, wrote in a newer bengali diary (with final pages torn out):

> "i have been with the family since 2000. i don't have any relations with those i knew as mother and sister. coming here, i have gained a new life. i don't want to live in a world where the aadom family will not be."

**5. the children's english notes.** at the end of the 36-page english text in the 1992 diary, two children, called "montu" (10) and "munni" (9) by selim, left short, all-caps notes in english that are nearly word for word identical down to the spelling errors (`DECLEARING`, `REIGINAL`, `MY LOW`, `BY MA DEPARTURE`):

> "1. I AM MONTU ABDUL ADAM / 2. I AM TEN YEARS OLD. / 3. I AM THE ANOTHER BODY OF ABDUL ADAM. / 4. I CAME THIS WORLD FOR WORK, ABOVE ALL IS ADAM. / 5. I AM DECLEARING THAT ALL RELIGION ARE FALSE... 9. I CAME TO THIS WORLD FOR FINISH MUHAMMAD EXISTANCE AND SAVE ALL HUMAN BEING FROM MUHAMMAD'S TRAP... 11. I DON'T OBEY ANY RULE OF MUHAMMADS ANY SOCIETY OR ANY LAW. 12. ABOVE OF ALL IS MY LOW, MY RULE... 14. I AM ALSO DECLEARING THAT, BY MA DEPARTURE MUHAMMAD'S ALL EXISTANCE, RELIGIONS AND WORSHIP PLACES WILL FINISH FOR EVER."

**6. the dates.** arif's diary records that the family first scheduled their death for 7 july 2007 (a date they attached cosmic meaning to), postponed it, attempted it again on 10 july (the seventh anniversary of darbesh's death), and carried it out on 11 july.

### the survivor's and relatives' versions
- **meherunnesa mobi (surviving daughter):** speaking from brahmanbaria, she rejected her father's anti-islamic doctrine, denied any conversion to christianity, and insisted the deaths were not suicide but a pre-planned murder carried out by neighbours and relatives over property and religious enmity.
- **abdul hannan (anwar darbesh's brother):** interviewed by *bdnews24* on 12 july 2007, he stated plainly that he believed his late brother's family members were suffering from mental illness.
- **neighbours:** told researchers that after 2000 the family severed almost all contact voluntarily, stayed indoors by day, and rarely interacted with anyone except when the young housemaid went to the market, denying the diary claims of continuous violent assaults.

### the researcher's version
nasima selim (2010) approached the case as both a physician and an anthropologist. she argued that reducing the notes purely to "madness" misses their cultural genealogy: darbesh's rejection of *shari'ah*, clerical authority, and ritual, and his claim that the human body itself is divine, drew directly on bengal's *be-shara* (anti-orthodox) and *fakiri* protest traditions. at the same time, she concluded that severe bereavement, poverty, social isolation, and unchallenged magical thinking tipped the household into a shared psychotic disorder (*folie à famille*), turning theological dissent into a fatal delusion of cosmic omnipotence.

## discussion
> a tidy label is a way of ending the conversation early.

### the four readings, bluntly
**1. shared psychosis (*folie à famille*).** this fits the internal structure of the notes: a grandiose founder claiming to be adam, relatives adopting his exact identity ("i am the another body of abdul adam"), nocturnal spirit channeling sessions, rigid isolation, and the shared belief that their physical deaths would annihilate all world religions.

**2. cult-driven group suicide.** this accounts for the months-long written preparation, the rescheduled dates (7, 10, 11 july), the coffins, and the trench. yet calling it a "cult" obscures how small and domestic it actually was: by 2007 there was no living prophet, no recruitment apparatus, and no institution outside one tin-shed house. it was a bereaved, impoverished family sustaining a dead father's worldview through notebooks and trance rituals. who gave the final operational order in july 2007, whether matriarch hena, oldest surviving son arif, or a consensus among the adult sisters, is never established in the notes.

**3. persecution, poverty and grief.** this was the family's own explanation, and parts of it are grounded in documented events: the 1985 hair-oil ban that impoverished nine people, the 2000 mob obstruction of darbesh's burial, and maehi's 2004 murder. yet "they experienced real hostility" and "villagers bombed their tin-shed house day and night for six years" are very different claims. no police report, neighbour testimony, or independent press account corroborates six years of bombings or knife attacks, and the diaries frame every local friction inside a cosmic war against "muhammad's agents." real trauma and persecutory delusion appear to have reinforced each other.

**4. murder.** mobi's allegation deserves to be recorded because she is the sole surviving immediate family member. however, it runs directly against the physical and documentary record: hundreds of pages of multi-authored diaries written over five months in two languages, near-identical english notes by the children, a trench dug inside the compound, coffins prepared in advance, and the train driver's eyewitness account of people walking onto the tracks and holding each other. furthermore, the diaries themselves reveal why mobi was excluded: the family regarded her as an enemy ("baby muhammad shaitan"). her claim reads most plausibly as a surviving sister's refusal to accept that her family chose to die, or an attempt to shield their memory from the stigma of suicide and apostasy.

### the part nobody likes: the children
the verbatim transcripts of "montu" and "munni's" notes in selim's paper settle a crucial question: the children did not compose independent philosophical testaments. their english notes are virtually identical templates, down to the same misspellings, appended right after their aunt's 36-page english text. whether dictated by an adult or copied line by line, they prove indoctrination, not consent. children aged eight to ten living inside a sealed household where every adult sleeps by day, channels dead relatives by night, and teaches that the outside world is controlled by demons have no exit and no alternative reality. calling their deaths "suicide" is a legal and journalistic convenience; ethically, the adults took them onto the tracks.

### what does not add up
even after checking the primary 2007 journalism and selim's 2010 paper, major contradictions remain in the public record:

- **who was the ninth victim—rahat or leena?** the 2007 press victim list names six adults (hena, akhtari, moon, morsheda, arif, rahat) and three children (shabnam, mou, molla). selim's 2010 paper, based on the suicide notes and field interviews, states that the nine dead were eight family members plus the 18-year-old housemaid/disciple ("reena" / leena), with only two children (aged 10 and 9) and one surviving adult son ("adil"). either the press misidentified one body at the scene (for instance, mistaking leena for a sibling, or miscounting rahat), or selim's anonymised genealogy compressed two brothers into one. no official post-mortem list is publicly available to settle this.
- **missing pages in the diaries.** selim notes that the final pages of both morsheda's ("monsura's") 109-page bengali diary and leena's ("reena's") diary were missing when she examined them. who removed them, and when, is unknown.
- **ages and descriptions.** press reports list the youngest victims as 10, 10, and 8, yet describe the two who died on the way to hospital as a young boy and a "teenage girl."
- **name variants across sources.** the same individuals appear under three different sets of names across wikipedia, *the daily star*, and selim's anonymised study (e.g., darbesh / anwar hossain / abdul adam; morsheda / murshida / monsura; molla / moula / montu; leena / reena; mobi / baby).
- **the trench and coffins.** both *bdnews24* and police reports mention a mass grave or trench dug on the western side of the house and prepared coffins, yet no source establishes who dug it, when it was dug, or why the family abandoned a house burial for the railway tracks on 11 july.
- **maehi's 2004 murder.** he was killed in dhaka in 2004 and found with forged university certificates, yet no source records whether his killers were ever identified or whether his murder had any connection to his forged credentials or his family's conflicts.

### what this case shows and does not show
it shows how a household can slowly seal itself off after economic ruin and bereavement until its own writings become the only reality it trusts. it shows how real social friction—a blocked burial in 2000, a murder in 2004—can be absorbed into an apocalyptic worldview where death feels like victory and reunion. and it shows how quickly public memory simplifies a messy record: newspapers turned it into "cult madness," neighbours blamed apostasy, the surviving sister called it murder, and later dramatizations, such as vicky zahed's 2024 series *chokro* and comparisons to india's 2018 burari deaths, inevitably smooth out the contradictions.

it does not show that religious unorthodoxy causes suicide, that the villagers were guilty of murder, or that every person on those tracks shared the same agency. the evidence supports a narrower, harder conclusion: a family under prolonged financial and emotional collapse, locked inside an inherited delusion, planned and carried out a collective death that included children who had no choice.
